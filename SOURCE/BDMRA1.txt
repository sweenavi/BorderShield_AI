# Part 1 – Introduction

## 5.1 Introduction

Route planning is one of the most critical functions of any transportation, logistics, or decision support system. Traditional navigation systems primarily focus on identifying the shortest path between two locations by considering only the physical distance or travel time. Although such methods are suitable for civilian navigation, they are insufficient for mission-oriented environments where operational safety is more important than simply reaching the destination through the shortest route.

In the BorderShield AI system, selecting an operational route requires the analysis of multiple real-time and static operational factors. A road that is geographically shorter may become unsuitable due to severe weather conditions, difficult terrain, temporary road closures, or mission-specific operational constraints. Therefore, selecting the shortest route alone cannot guarantee mission success or operational safety.

To overcome these limitations, BorderShield AI adopts a hybrid routing framework known as the **BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA)**. Instead of considering only distance, the framework evaluates every road segment using multiple operational datasets before determining the optimal route.

The proposed framework combines two major components. The first component is a **Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine**, which analyzes operational datasets such as Road Master, Terrain Master, Weather Master, Geographic Master, and Mission Information to calculate the operational cost associated with each road segment. The second component is the **Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm**, which uses these dynamically generated operational costs as edge weights to identify the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.

By separating the decision-making process from the routing process, the proposed framework provides dynamic, adaptive, and intelligent route recommendations while retaining the computational efficiency and reliability of the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm.

Consequently, BorderShield AI is capable of recommending routes that are not merely the shortest, but also the safest and most operationally suitable according to current environmental and mission conditions.

---

## 5.2 Objective of the Algorithm

The primary objective of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA) is to determine the safest and most operationally suitable route between a selected source and destination by considering multiple operational parameters rather than relying solely on geographical distance.

The algorithm is designed to achieve the following objectives:

* Build a weighted graph representing the operational road network.
* Collect operational information from multiple datasets.
* Evaluate every road segment using multiple risk factors.
* Dynamically calculate the operational cost of each road.
* Assign the calculated operational cost as the edge weight.
* Execute the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm on the dynamically weighted graph.
* Recommend the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.
* Automatically adapt route recommendations whenever operational conditions change.

This approach enables the system to perform intelligent route planning under dynamic operational environments while maintaining computational efficiency.

---

## 5.3 Problem Statement

Conventional shortest-path algorithms assume that the cost of travelling through a road segment remains fixed and is generally represented by its physical distance or travel time. However, in mission-oriented environments, the suitability of a road depends on numerous operational factors that continuously change over time.

For example, a road may initially be safe under clear weather conditions but become highly risky after heavy snowfall or reduced visibility. Similarly, a shorter mountain road may present greater operational challenges than a longer plain route.

Therefore, relying exclusively on distance-based routing may result in unsafe route recommendations.

BorderShield AI addresses this limitation by evaluating multiple operational datasets before route computation. Instead of assigning fixed edge weights, the system dynamically calculates an operational cost for every road segment based on current environmental and mission conditions.

This dynamic operational cost enables the routing process to adapt automatically to changing operational scenarios, ensuring that recommended routes remain both safe and efficient.

---

## 5.4 Need for a Dynamic Routing Framework

The operational environment considered by BorderShield AI is highly dynamic. Weather conditions, terrain accessibility, road availability, and mission priorities may change at any time. Consequently, the routing framework must be capable of adapting to these changes without requiring manual intervention.

A dynamic routing framework is required because:

* Road conditions may change due to weather or operational incidents.
* Terrain difficulty varies across different geographical regions.
* Mission priorities influence route selection.
* Different vehicle types may require different routes.
* Temporary road closures must immediately affect routing decisions.
* New operational information should automatically update future recommendations.

To satisfy these requirements, BorderShield AI utilizes a dynamic routing framework in which road costs are recalculated whenever operational datasets are updated. The routing algorithm subsequently generates a new optimal route using the updated operational costs.

As a result, route recommendations remain synchronized with the latest operational information throughout the mission lifecycle.

# 5.5 Algorithm Selection Process

Selecting an appropriate routing algorithm is one of the most critical design decisions in the BorderShield AI system. Since the primary objective of the project is to recommend the safest operational route rather than merely the shortest route, it was necessary to evaluate several graph traversal and shortest-path algorithms before selecting the final routing architecture.

The selected algorithm needed to satisfy the following project requirements:

* Handle weighted road networks.
* Support dynamic operational conditions.
* Allow integration of multiple datasets.
* Produce reliable route recommendations.
* Be computationally efficient.
* Be easy to implement and explain in a B.Tech project.

To determine the most suitable solution, several pathfinding algorithms and routing approaches were studied and compared.

---

# 5.6 Algorithms Considered During Research

The following algorithms and routing approaches were evaluated during the design phase.

## 1. Breadth First Search (BFS)

Breadth First Search is one of the simplest graph traversal algorithms. It explores neighboring nodes level by level until the destination node is found.

### Advantages

* Simple implementation.
* Fast for unweighted graphs.
* Always finds the minimum number of edges.

### Limitations

* Does not support weighted graphs.
* Cannot differentiate between safe and unsafe roads.
* Considers every road equally important.

### Suitability for BorderShield AI

Not suitable because the operational road network contains different road lengths and varying operational risks.

**Decision:** Rejected

---

## 2. Depth First Search (DFS)

Depth First Search explores one path completely before returning to explore alternative paths.

### Advantages

* Easy to implement.
* Useful for graph traversal.

### Limitations

* Does not guarantee the optimal path.
* Does not support weighted routing.
* Can produce inefficient routes.

### Suitability for BorderShield AI

Not suitable because the objective is optimal route recommendation rather than graph traversal.

**Decision:** Rejected

---

## 3. Bellman-Ford Algorithm

Bellman-Ford calculates shortest paths even when negative edge weights are present.

### Advantages

* Supports negative edge weights.
* Detects negative cycles.

### Limitations

* Higher computational complexity.
* Slower than Dijkstra for positive-weight graphs.
* Negative edge weights are unnecessary for this project.

### Suitability for BorderShield AI

Operational road costs are always positive.

Therefore, Bellman-Ford provides no practical advantage.

**Decision:** Rejected

---

## 4. Floyd-Warshall Algorithm

Floyd-Warshall computes the shortest paths between every pair of nodes in a graph.

### Advantages

* Produces all-pairs shortest paths.
* Useful for complete network analysis.

### Limitations

* High computational complexity.
* Calculates many unnecessary routes.
* Inefficient for single mission requests.

### Suitability for BorderShield AI

Each mission requires only one source and one destination.

Therefore, calculating every possible path is unnecessary.

**Decision:** Rejected

---

## 5. A* Algorithm

The A* Algorithm improves shortest-path computation by using heuristics to estimate the remaining distance to the destination.

### Advantages

* Faster than Dijkstra for large road networks.
* Efficient when a suitable heuristic exists.

### Limitations

* Requires an accurate heuristic.
* Route quality depends on heuristic selection.
* Increased implementation complexity.

### Suitability for BorderShield AI

The current operational network contains approximately 20 operational locations and a limited number of road segments. The computational overhead of Dijkstra is already sufficiently low.

Therefore, the additional complexity of heuristic design is not justified.

**Decision:** Not Selected

---

## 6. Standard Dijkstra Algorithm

The Dijkstra Algorithm computes the shortest path between a source and destination in a weighted graph.

### Advantages

* Reliable.
* Efficient.
* Well-established.
* Easy to validate.
* Suitable for positive edge weights.
* Widely used in transportation systems.

### Limitations

By default, Dijkstra assumes that every edge weight is already known.

Traditionally,

```text
Edge Weight = Distance
```

This means the algorithm does not understand:

* Weather
* Terrain
* Road Status
* Mission Priority
* Operational Constraints

It simply minimizes numerical edge weights.

### Suitability for BorderShield AI

The routing capability of Dijkstra is excellent.

However, the edge weights must represent operational conditions rather than only distance.

Therefore, Dijkstra alone is insufficient.

**Decision:** Selected as the Core Routing Algorithm.

---

# 5.7 Understanding Multi-Criteria Path Planning

During the research process, another important concept known as **Multi-Criteria Path Planning** was studied.

Initially, it appeared to be an alternative routing algorithm.

However, a detailed analysis revealed that this assumption is incorrect.

Multi-Criteria Path Planning is **not** a single routing algorithm.

Instead, it is a routing methodology or optimization framework that evaluates multiple decision factors before selecting a route.

Within this framework, different shortest-path algorithms can be used, including:

* Standard Dijkstra Algorithm
* A* Algorithm
* Bellman-Ford Algorithm
* Genetic Algorithms
* Ant Colony Optimization
* Particle Swarm Optimization

Therefore, simply stating that the project uses "Multi-Criteria Path Planning" does not explain how the final path is computed.

The actual pathfinding algorithm must still be specified.

---

# 5.8 Key Design Observation

The detailed study of Dijkstra Algorithm and Multi-Criteria Path Planning led to one important observation.

The routing algorithm and the decision-making process are two independent components.

The routing algorithm determines **how** the path is searched.

The decision-making process determines **how** the cost of every road is calculated.

Therefore,

the routing algorithm itself does not need to understand operational conditions.

Instead, it only requires numerical edge weights.

Those edge weights can be generated by a separate decision-making module.

This observation became the foundation of the proposed BorderShield AI routing architecture.

---

# 5.9 Final Design Decision

Based on the comparative study, the following architecture was selected.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm is used exclusively for route computation.

A separate Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine is responsible for evaluating operational datasets and calculating dynamic operational costs for every road segment.

These operational costs become the edge weights of the weighted graph.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm then computes the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.

This hybrid approach combines intelligent decision making with a reliable shortest-path algorithm while maintaining computational efficiency and implementation simplicity.

---

| Standard Dijkstra Algorithm  | Selected                   | Reliable, efficient, and suitable for weighted routing.                |
| Multi-Criteria Path Planning | Adopted as Design Strategy | Used for dynamic operational cost calculation rather than pathfinding. |

---

# 5.11 Conclusion of Algorithm Selection

After evaluating several routing algorithms and decision-making approaches, it was concluded that no single algorithm independently satisfies all the operational requirements of BorderShield AI.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm provides an efficient shortest-path solution but does not consider operational conditions.

Conversely, Multi-Criteria Path Planning enables the evaluation of multiple operational parameters but does not itself define the shortest-path computation.

Therefore, BorderShield AI adopts a hybrid architecture that combines the strengths of both approaches.

The Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine dynamically computes operational costs for each road segment, while the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm identifies the route with the minimum cumulative operational cost.

This combination forms the foundation of the **BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA)**, which serves as the core routing framework of the proposed system.

# 5.12 Understanding the Routing Architecture

After selecting the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm as the core routing algorithm and Multi-Criteria Path Planning as the decision-making strategy, the next step was to determine how both concepts could be integrated into a single routing framework.

Initially, it appeared that Dynamic Weighted Dijkstra might be an entirely new routing algorithm. However, a detailed analysis revealed that this assumption is incorrect.

The routing algorithm itself remains the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm. The innovation of the proposed framework lies not in modifying the routing algorithm but in dynamically calculating the edge weights before the routing process begins.

Therefore, the routing architecture consists of two independent components that work sequentially.

1. **Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine**
2. **Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm**

The first component performs intelligent decision making, while the second component performs shortest-path computation.

---

# 5.13 Difference Between Routing Algorithm and Risk Evaluation

A common misunderstanding is that Dynamic Weighted Dijkstra represents a completely different shortest-path algorithm.

Technically, this is not correct.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm has remained unchanged for decades and is responsible only for computing the shortest path within a weighted graph.

It does not understand operational concepts such as:

* Weather
* Terrain
* Road Conditions
* Mission Priority
* Vehicle Type
* Visibility
* Snowfall
* Road Closures

Instead, the algorithm requires only one input for every road segment:

> **A numerical edge weight.**

Its objective is simply to identify the path whose total edge weight is minimum.

Therefore, the intelligence of BorderShield AI is not embedded inside Dijkstra itself.

Instead, intelligence is introduced before Dijkstra executes by calculating meaningful edge weights from multiple operational datasets.

---

# 5.14 Relationship Between Risk Evaluation and Routing

The routing framework separates the overall process into two independent stages.

The first stage evaluates operational conditions.

The second stage computes the optimal route.

This relationship can be represented as follows:

```text
                Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine
                             │
                             ▼
              Calculates Operational Cost of Every Road
                             │
                             ▼
              Generates Dynamic Edge Weights
                             │
                             ▼
            Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm
                             │
                             ▼
             Generates Best Operational Route
```

This separation ensures that each component performs a specialized task.

The Risk Evaluation Engine performs intelligent analysis.

The Dijkstra Algorithm performs efficient route computation.

---

# 5.15 Formation of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA)

Based on the architectural study, the proposed routing framework is formed by integrating the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm with a Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine.

Mathematically, the framework can be represented as:

```text
BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA)

=

Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine

+

Dynamic Edge Weight Generation

+

Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm
```

It is important to note that BDMRA is **not** a newly invented shortest-path algorithm.

Instead, it is a hybrid routing framework developed specifically for BorderShield AI by combining an established shortest-path algorithm with a dynamic operational decision-making mechanism.

---

# 5.16 Why Dynamic Edge Weights Are Required

Traditional shortest-path systems assign a fixed weight to every road segment.

For example,

```text
Edge Weight = Distance
```

Such an approach assumes that road conditions remain constant throughout the mission.

However, operational environments are highly dynamic.

Road conditions change because of:

* Weather
* Snowfall
* Terrain accessibility
* Operational incidents
* Temporary road closures
* Mission requirements

If the edge weight remains fixed, the routing algorithm cannot adapt to changing conditions.

Therefore, BorderShield AI replaces fixed edge weights with dynamically generated operational costs.

Instead of using only distance,

the proposed framework uses:

```text
Edge Weight = Dynamic Operational Cost
```

The operational cost is calculated from multiple operational datasets and reflects the current suitability of travelling through each road segment.

---

# 5.17 Why the Framework Is Called "Dynamic"

The routing framework is described as dynamic because the edge weights continuously adapt to changing operational conditions.

Consider a road segment connecting two operational locations.

Initially,

| Parameter   | Value      |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| Weather     | Heavy Snow |
| Visibility  | Poor       |
| Road Status | Open       |
| Terrain     | Glacier    |

After evaluating these conditions,

the Risk Evaluation Engine computes:

```text
Operational Cost = 48
```

This value becomes the edge weight used by the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm.

Suppose weather conditions improve one hour later.

The updated information becomes:

| Parameter   | Value   |
| ----------- | ------- |
| Weather     | Clear   |
| Visibility  | Good    |
| Road Status | Open    |
| Terrain     | Glacier |

The Risk Evaluation Engine now computes:

```text
Operational Cost = 18
```

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm itself has not changed.

Only the edge weight has changed.

When the algorithm executes again, it automatically considers the updated operational cost and may recommend a different route.

This automatic adaptation to changing operational conditions is the defining characteristic of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm.

# 5.18 Overall Working Principle

The complete routing process follows a sequential workflow.

First, the system receives the mission request.

Next, all operational datasets are loaded.

The Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine then evaluates every road segment using the available operational information.

After calculating the operational cost of each road, the system constructs a dynamically weighted graph.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm subsequently computes the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.

Finally, the system generates the complete route recommendation along with operational information such as total distance, estimated travel time, and overall route risk.

This workflow allows BorderShield AI to integrate intelligent decision making with reliable shortest-path computation.

---

# 5.19 Advantages of Separating Decision Making and Routing

Separating risk evaluation from route computation provides several important advantages.

* The routing algorithm remains simple and computationally efficient.
* New operational parameters can be added without modifying the routing algorithm.
* Dynamic operational conditions automatically influence route recommendations.
* The framework is modular and easy to maintain.
* Risk evaluation logic can evolve independently of the shortest-path algorithm.
* The architecture is scalable for future expansion.

This modular design also improves software maintainability because each component performs a clearly defined responsibility.

The BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm should be interpreted as a routing framework rather than a newly invented shortest-path algorithm.

The framework combines:

* Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation

Each component performs an independent function, collectively enabling intelligent route recommendation under dynamic operational conditions.

The BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA) is a hybrid routing framework that integrates a Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine with the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm. The Risk Evaluation Engine analyzes operational datasets, including Geographic Master, Road Master, Terrain Master, Weather Master, Mission Information, and future operational updates, to calculate a dynamic operational cost for every road segment. These operational costs are assigned as edge weights within a weighted graph. The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm then processes the dynamically weighted graph to determine the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost, thereby recommending the safest and most operationally suitable route between the selected source and destination.
