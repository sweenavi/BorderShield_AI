Final Feasibility / Constraint Points

### A. Route Availability Constraints

1. **Road Status**

   * Open → route available
   * Restricted → route available with restriction/treatment
   * Blocked/Closed → **route unavailable**

2. **Route Accessibility**

   * Source and destination must be connected to the available road/air graph.
   * Disconnected route → **infeasible**

3. **Road Type Compatibility**

   * Check whether the selected transport mode is permitted/suitable for that road type.

---

### B. Transport Compatibility Constraints

4. **Vehicle Suitability**

   * Compare mission transport/vehicle type with `vehicle_suitability`.
   * Example:

     * Snow Vehicle → Snow-compatible route
     * 4×4 → 4×4-compatible route
     * Specialized Vehicle → specialized route
   * Incompatible → **infeasible or restricted**

5. **Surface Compatibility**

   * Check transport mode against:

     * Asphalt
     * Gravel
     * Compacted Snow
     * Snow/Ice
   * Example: normal vehicle + Snow/Ice → potentially restricted/infeasible depending on vehicle capability.

6. **Terrain Compatibility**

   * Check whether the selected mode/vehicle is appropriate for the terrain type.
   * Example:

     * Glacier
     * Snow
     * Rocky Mountain
   * Compatibility should be determined separately from Terrain Risk.

---

### C. Environmental Feasibility Constraints

7. **Water-Crossing Feasibility**

   * Water Crossing = No → no constraint.
   * Water Crossing = Yes → check whether the selected transport mode/vehicle can cross.
   * If not capable → **infeasible**.
   * If capable → route remains available.

8. **Avalanche Constraint**

   * Very High avalanche condition can trigger an operational restriction.
   * If the current operational rule says the route cannot be used → **remove edge**.
   * Otherwise retain with appropriate treatment.

9. **Landslide Constraint**

   * High landslide condition can trigger route restriction/closure depending on operational status.
   * Do not automatically convert Landslide Risk into a closure.

10. **Visibility Constraint**

* Extremely poor visibility can trigger a transport-specific operational restriction.
* Example:

  * Ground movement may have one threshold.
  * Air movement may have another.
* The visibility risk score itself remains unchanged.

11. **Extreme Weather Constraint**

* Certain combinations of weather conditions can make a route temporarily infeasible.
* Example:

  * Blizzard + very poor visibility + severe snowfall.
* This should be implemented as an **operational rule**, not as another risk factor.

---

### D. Mission Constraints

12. **Mission Type Compatibility**

* Check whether the route/mode is appropriate for the mission.
* Example:

  * Supply → requires appropriate ground/air transport capability.
  * Patrol → may permit routes unsuitable for heavy vehicles.
  * Emergency → may require faster/accessible alternatives.

13. **Transport Mode**

* Ground
* Air
* Foot
* Any future supported mode

Each mode should have its own feasibility rules.

14. **Mission Priority**

* Priority should **not change environmental risk**.
* It can affect operational routing policy.
* Example: a high-priority mission may allow a restricted route if operational rules permit it, rather than treating the route as inherently safer.

---

### E. Infrastructure / Operational Constraints

15. **Checkpoint Availability**

* If a checkpoint is mandatory for the route and unavailable → route may become restricted/infeasible.
* Otherwise it can contribute to ETA/operational processing.

16. **Helipad Availability**

* For air missions:

  * required helipad exists
  * helipad operational/available
  * route can connect to it

17. **Air Route Availability**

* Air missions should use the **Air Route Master** rather than the land-road graph.

18. **Rest Point / Operational Stop Availability**

* If a mission requires designated rest/refueling/operational points, verify their availability.
* This should primarily affect feasibility/ETA, not environmental risk.

---

## Final conceptual distinction

Your system should make decisions in this order:

```text
          ROUTE
            ↓
     Is it available?
            ↓
       [CONSTRAINT]
            ↓
     Is it feasible for
     this mission/mode?
            ↓
       [FEASIBILITY]
            ↓
     What is its environmental
     operational risk?
            ↓
          [ORAM]
            ↓
     Generate Operational Cost
            ↓
       Dynamic Edge Weight
            ↓
    Standard Dijkstra
```

And one particularly important rule:

> **A constraint should remove or restrict an edge; it should not automatically increase the 0–100 environmental risk score.**

That keeps your **standardization work frozen and clean**, while mission-specific behavior is handled by the separate feasibility layer.
