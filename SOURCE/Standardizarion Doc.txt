# Complete Risk Standardization Framework

The purpose of this stage is to take the raw values stored in the Weather Master, Terrain Master, and Road Master and convert only the parameters that actually contribute to operational risk into a common 0–100 risk scale.

The three risk categories remain:

WEATHER → Weather Risk
TERRAIN → Terrain Risk
ROAD → Road Risk

Historical Risk is not included.

The system does not fabricate historical accident, closure, or incident information because no separate historical dataset is available.

The Geographic Master is also not a risk dataset. It provides the original geographical coordinates and related geographic information from which the operational network and other datasets are derived.

Distance is important for the system, but it is not itself a risk factor. It will later be used for ETA and route/cost calculations.

Road Status is not part of the risk formula. It is handled separately during route selection.

Checkpoints are used for ETA and operational information.

Helipads are not risk factors.

Rest Points are used for ETA and operational information.

Observation Towers are not risk factors.

Air Routes are not risk factors.

Extra columns in the datasets should not automatically be deleted from the database. They may remain for storage, information, visualization, future functionality, or operational reference. Only the parameters that directly contribute to the risk model are passed into MCREE.

# 1. Weather Risk

Weather Risk is calculated from weather parameters that can directly affect road traversal.

The current candidate weather parameters are:

Temperature

Snowfall

Visibility

Wind Speed

Humidity

The important principle is:

A parameter being present in the Weather Master does not automatically mean that it contributes to risk.

Each parameter must have a defensible operational relationship with road traversal.

---

## 1.1 Temperature

Parameter:

Temperature

Unit:

°C

Risk direction:

Extreme conditions → higher risk

Standardization method:

Piecewise/threshold function

Output:

0–100

The temperature value received from the Weather API or Weather Master is converted into a standardized Temperature Risk score.

The exact thresholds are not yet frozen.

They must be researched and validated.

The reason for using a threshold/piecewise approach is that operational risk does not necessarily increase linearly across all temperatures.

For example, a temperature that is comfortable for normal operation may have very low risk, while extremely low temperatures may substantially increase operational difficulty.

Therefore, the model should not simply assume:

Temperature × constant

Instead, temperature should be mapped according to operationally meaningful ranges.

---

## 1.2 Snowfall

Parameter:

Snowfall

Unit:

mm or category, depending on the source

Risk direction:

Higher snowfall → higher risk

Standardization method:

Lookup + threshold mapping

Output:

0–100

Snowfall is one of the most important weather parameters because increasing snowfall can affect:

Road surface

Traction

Visibility

Vehicle movement

Road accessibility

Operational reliability

The raw snowfall value is therefore converted into a standardized Snowfall Risk score.

Example structure:

No/very low snowfall
↓
Low Risk

Moderate snowfall
↓
Moderate Risk

Heavy snowfall
↓
High Risk

Very severe snowfall
↓
Critical Risk

However, the actual numerical values must be researched and validated before they are frozen.

An earlier value such as:

Heavy Snow = 90

was only an illustrative example and must not automatically become the final value.

meters

0–100

↓
Higher risk

↓

Example conceptual structure:

↓
Low Risk

↓
Moderate Risk

Poor visibility
↓
High Risk

Extremely poor visibility
↓
Critical Risk

The exact threshold values must be validated.

# 16. Relationship Between Standardization, AHP, and Routing

These are three different responsibilities.

Standardization answers:

"How risky is this individual parameter on a common 0–100 scale?"

AHP answers:

"How important is Weather compared with Terrain and Road?"

Operational Risk answers:

"What is the combined risk of this road segment?"

Routing answers:

"Among the connected roads, which route has the best total operational cost according to the current conditions?"

Therefore:

Raw Data
↓
Standardization
↓
Category Risks
↓
AHP
↓
Operational Risk
↓
Operational Cost
↓
Dynamic Edge Weight
↓
Routing

# 17. What We Should Not Do

We should not:

- Assign arbitrary risk numbers without justification.
- Include every database column in the risk formula.
- Treat coordinates as risk.
- Treat distance as risk.
- Treat Road Status as a risk factor.
- Create fake accident or closure history.
- Include historical risk when there is no actual historical dataset.
- Give every weather parameter a risk contribution automatically.
- Give every terrain column a risk contribution automatically.
- Give every road column a risk contribution automatically.
- Apply AHP directly to raw values.
- Recalculate AHP weights during every mission.
- Treat illustrative values as final research values.
