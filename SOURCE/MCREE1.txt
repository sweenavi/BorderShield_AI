# Chapter 6: Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine (MCREE)

---

# 6.1 Introduction

The BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA) relies on the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm for route computation. However, the Dijkstra Algorithm requires every road segment to have an edge weight before it can determine the optimal route.

Instead of using physical distance as the edge weight, BorderShield AI introduces a **Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine (MCREE)**.

The purpose of this engine is to evaluate every road segment using multiple operational datasets and convert those parameters into a single operational cost.

This operational cost becomes the edge weight supplied to the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm.

Therefore, the Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine acts as the intelligence layer of the BorderShield AI routing framework.

---

# 6.2 Purpose of the Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine

The primary purpose of the engine is to determine the operational suitability of every road segment before route computation begins.

Unlike traditional navigation systems that consider only geographical distance, this engine evaluates several operational factors that directly influence mission safety and route reliability.

The calculated operational cost represents the overall difficulty and risk associated with travelling through a specific road segment.

This enables the routing system to recommend routes that are operationally safer rather than simply shorter.

---

# 6.3 Why Risk Evaluation Is Required

In conventional routing systems,

```text
Edge Weight = Distance
```

This assumption is appropriate for civilian navigation because road conditions generally remain stable.

However, BorderShield AI operates within an environment where road conditions continuously change due to operational and environmental factors.

For example,

Road A

* Distance : 10 km
* Heavy Snow
* Poor Visibility
* Glacier Surface

Road B

* Distance : 15 km
* Clear Weather
* Good Visibility
* Asphalt Surface

Although Road A is geographically shorter, it presents significantly greater operational challenges.

Therefore, distance alone cannot represent the actual suitability of a road.

To solve this limitation, BorderShield AI replaces distance with an operational cost calculated using multiple criteria.

---

# 6.4 Multi-Criteria Evaluation Concept

The Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine combines information obtained from multiple datasets.

Instead of making decisions based on a single parameter, every road segment is evaluated using all available operational information.

The engine follows the principle that:

> **The operational suitability of a road depends on the combined effect of multiple parameters rather than any single parameter alone.**

This allows the system to produce more realistic route recommendations.

# 6.7 Working Principle of the Risk Evaluation Engine

The engine evaluates one road segment at a time.

For each road,

the system performs the following operations:

### Step 1

Identify the road segment.

Example:

Road R023

L05 → L06

---

### Step 2

Collect information from all connected datasets.

Example

Road Master

* Distance = 12 km
* Road Status = Open

Terrain Master

* Terrain = Glacier
* Surface = Ice

Weather Master

* Snowfall = Heavy
* Visibility = Poor

Mission Information

* Mission Priority = High
* Vehicle = Supply Vehicle

---

### Step 3

Analyze every operational parameter.

Each parameter contributes to the overall operational suitability of the road.

---

### Step 4

Generate a single operational cost.

Example

```text
Road R023

↓

Operational Cost = 48
```

---

### Step 5

Assign the operational cost as the edge weight.

The road is now ready for routing.

---

# 6.8 Output of the Risk Evaluation Engine

For every road segment,

the engine generates:

* Operational Cost
* Edge Weight
* Risk Category
* Updated Road Status

These outputs are forwarded to the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm.

---

# 6.9 Interaction with BDMRA

The Risk Evaluation Engine does not determine the route.

Its responsibility ends after assigning the operational cost.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm then receives these operational costs and computes the optimal route.

Therefore,

the routing framework operates as follows:

```text
Road Information
        │
Terrain Information
        │
Weather Information
        │
Mission Information
        │
Operational Updates
        │
        ▼
Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine
        │
        ▼
Operational Cost Generation
        │
        ▼
Dynamic Edge Weight Assignment
        │
        ▼
BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm
        │
        ▼
Optimal Route Recommendation
```

# 6.11 Conclusion

The Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine represents the intelligence layer of BorderShield AI.

Rather than modifying the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm, the proposed framework improves routing quality by generating dynamic operational costs from multiple operational datasets.

This separation between decision making and route computation creates a modular, scalable, and efficient routing framework capable of adapting to continuously changing operational environments.

The generated operational costs form the foundation upon which the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm computes the safest and most operationally suitable route.
