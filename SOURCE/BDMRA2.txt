BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA)

PART 1

================================================================================
5.1 Introduction
================================================================================

Route planning is one of the most critical functions of any transportation,
logistics, or decision support system. Traditional navigation systems primarily
focus on identifying the shortest path between two locations by considering
only the physical distance or travel time. Although such methods are suitable
for civilian navigation, they are insufficient for mission-oriented
environments where operational safety is more important than simply reaching
the destination through the shortest route.

In the BorderShield AI system, selecting an operational route requires the
analysis of multiple real-time and static operational factors. A road that is
geographically shorter may become unsuitable due to severe weather conditions,
difficult terrain, temporary road closures, or mission-specific operational
constraints. Therefore, selecting the shortest route alone cannot guarantee
mission success or operational safety.

To overcome these limitations, BorderShield AI adopts a hybrid routing
framework known as the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm
(BDMRA). Instead of considering only distance, the framework evaluates every
road segment using multiple operational datasets before determining the optimal
route.

The proposed framework combines two major components.

The first component is a Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine (MCREE), which
analyzes operational datasets such as the Road Master, Terrain Master, Weather
Master, Geographic Master, and Mission Information to calculate the operational
cost associated with each road segment.

The second component is the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm, which
uses these dynamically generated operational costs as edge weights to identify
the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.

By separating the decision-making process from the routing process, the
proposed framework provides dynamic, adaptive, and intelligent route
recommendations while retaining the computational efficiency and reliability of
the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm.

Consequently, BorderShield AI is capable of recommending routes that are not
merely the shortest, but also the safest and most operationally suitable
according to current environmental and mission conditions.

================================================================================
5.2 Objective of the Algorithm
================================================================================

The primary objective of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing
Algorithm (BDMRA) is to determine the safest and most operationally suitable
route between a selected source and destination by considering multiple
operational parameters rather than relying solely on geographical distance.

The algorithm is designed to achieve the following objectives:

• Build a weighted graph representing the operational road network.

• Collect operational information from multiple datasets.

• Evaluate every road segment using multiple risk factors.

• Dynamically calculate the operational cost of each road.

• Assign the calculated operational cost as the edge weight.

• Execute the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm on the dynamically
  weighted graph.

• Recommend the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.

• Automatically adapt route recommendations whenever operational conditions
  change.

This approach enables the system to perform intelligent route planning under
dynamic operational environments while maintaining computational efficiency.

================================================================================
5.3 Problem Statement
================================================================================

Conventional shortest-path algorithms assume that the cost of travelling
through a road segment remains fixed and is generally represented by its
physical distance or travel time.

However, in mission-oriented environments, the suitability of a road depends on
numerous operational factors that continuously change over time.

For example, a road may initially be safe under clear weather conditions but
become highly risky after heavy snowfall or reduced visibility. Similarly, a
shorter mountain road may present greater operational challenges than a longer
plain route.

Therefore, relying exclusively on distance-based routing may result in unsafe
route recommendations.

BorderShield AI addresses this limitation by evaluating multiple operational
datasets before route computation.

Instead of assigning fixed edge weights, the system dynamically calculates an
operational cost for every road segment based on current environmental and
mission conditions.

This dynamic operational cost enables the routing process to adapt
automatically to changing operational scenarios, ensuring that recommended
routes remain both safe and efficient.

================================================================================
5.4 Need for a Dynamic Routing Framework
================================================================================

The operational environment considered by BorderShield AI is highly dynamic.

Weather conditions, terrain accessibility, road availability, and mission
priorities may change at any time.

Consequently, the routing framework must be capable of adapting to these
changes without requiring manual intervention.

A dynamic routing framework is required because:

• Road conditions may change due to weather or operational incidents.

• Terrain difficulty varies across different geographical regions.

• Mission priorities influence route selection.

• Different vehicle types may require different routes.

• Temporary road closures must immediately affect routing decisions.

• New operational information should automatically update future
  recommendations.

To satisfy these requirements, BorderShield AI utilizes a dynamic routing
framework in which road costs are recalculated whenever operational datasets
are updated.

The routing algorithm subsequently generates a new optimal route using the
updated operational costs.

As a result, route recommendations remain synchronized with the latest
operational information throughout the mission lifecycle.

5.5 Algorithm Selection Process
================================================================================

Selecting an appropriate routing algorithm is one of the most critical design
decisions in the BorderShield AI system. Since the primary objective of the
project is to recommend the safest operational route rather than merely the
shortest route, it was necessary to evaluate several graph traversal and
shortest-path algorithms before selecting the final routing architecture.

The selected algorithm needed to satisfy the following project requirements:

• Handle weighted road networks.

• Support dynamic operational conditions.

• Allow integration of multiple datasets.

• Produce reliable route recommendations.

• Be computationally efficient.

• Be easy to implement, understand, and explain in an academic project.

To determine the most suitable solution, several pathfinding algorithms and
routing approaches were studied, analyzed, and compared.

================================================================================
5.6 Algorithms Considered During Research
================================================================================

During the research phase, multiple graph traversal and shortest-path
algorithms were evaluated based on their characteristics, computational
efficiency, and suitability for the operational requirements of BorderShield
AI.

The following algorithms were considered before selecting the final routing
framework.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6.1 Breadth First Search (BFS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Breadth First Search (BFS) is one of the simplest graph traversal algorithms.
It explores neighboring nodes level by level until the destination node is
reached.

Advantages

• Simple implementation.

• Fast for unweighted graphs.

• Guarantees the minimum number of edges between two nodes.

Limitations

• Does not support weighted graphs.

• Treats every road as equally important.

• Cannot differentiate between safe and unsafe roads.

Suitability for BorderShield AI

Since the operational road network contains roads with different distances,
terrain conditions, and operational risks, BFS cannot represent the actual
cost of travelling through each road.

Decision

Rejected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6.2 Depth First Search (DFS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Depth First Search (DFS) explores one path completely before backtracking to
search alternative paths.

Advantages

• Easy to implement.

• Useful for graph traversal and connectivity analysis.

Limitations

• Does not guarantee the optimal path.

• Does not support weighted routing.

• May produce inefficient routes.

Suitability for BorderShield AI

The objective of BorderShield AI is to recommend the most suitable operational
route rather than simply traverse the graph.

Decision

Rejected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6.3 Bellman-Ford Algorithm
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Bellman-Ford Algorithm computes shortest paths even when negative edge
weights are present.

Advantages

• Supports negative edge weights.

• Detects negative cycles.

Limitations

• Higher computational complexity than Dijkstra.

• Slower execution.

• Negative edge weights are unnecessary for this project.

Suitability for BorderShield AI

Operational road costs are always positive values.

Therefore, Bellman-Ford provides no practical advantage for the proposed
routing framework.

Decision

Rejected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6.4 Floyd-Warshall Algorithm
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Floyd-Warshall Algorithm computes the shortest paths between every pair of
nodes within a graph.

Advantages

• Produces all-pairs shortest paths.

• Useful for complete network analysis.

Limitations

• High computational complexity.

• Computes many unnecessary routes.

• Inefficient for individual mission requests.

Suitability for BorderShield AI

Each mission requires routing between only one source and one destination.

Calculating shortest paths between every pair of operational locations would
consume unnecessary computational resources.

Decision

Rejected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6.5 A* Algorithm
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The A* Algorithm improves shortest-path computation by introducing heuristics
to estimate the remaining distance toward the destination.

Advantages

• Faster than Dijkstra on very large graphs.

• Efficient when an accurate heuristic exists.

Limitations

• Requires heuristic design.

• Route quality depends on heuristic accuracy.

• More complex implementation.

Suitability for BorderShield AI

The proposed operational network consists of approximately twenty operational
locations connected through a manageable number of road segments.

For this network size, the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm provides sufficient
performance without requiring heuristic optimization.

Decision

Not Selected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6.6 Standard Dijkstra Algorithm
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm computes the shortest path between a source and
destination within a weighted graph.

Advantages

• Reliable.

• Efficient.

• Widely accepted.

• Computationally stable.

• Easy to validate.

• Suitable for positive edge weights.

Limitations

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm assumes that every edge already has a numerical
weight.

Traditionally,

Edge Weight = Distance

The algorithm does not understand:

• Weather

• Terrain

• Road Status

• Visibility

• Mission Priority

• Vehicle Type

• Operational Constraints

Its responsibility is limited to identifying the path having the minimum
cumulative edge weight.

Suitability for BorderShield AI

The routing capability of Dijkstra is excellent.

However, the edge weights must represent operational conditions instead of
physical distance alone.

Decision

Selected as the Core Routing Algorithm.

================================================================================
5.7 Understanding Multi-Criteria Path Planning
================================================================================

During the literature survey, another important concept known as Multi-Criteria
Path Planning was studied.

Initially, it appeared to be an alternative shortest-path algorithm.

However, detailed analysis revealed that this assumption is incorrect.

Multi-Criteria Path Planning is not a single routing algorithm.

Instead, it is a decision-making framework that evaluates multiple operational
criteria before determining the best route.

Different shortest-path algorithms can operate within this framework,
including:

• Standard Dijkstra Algorithm

• A* Algorithm

• Bellman-Ford Algorithm

• Genetic Algorithms

• Ant Colony Optimization

• Particle Swarm Optimization

Therefore, simply stating that a system uses Multi-Criteria Path Planning is
academically incomplete because the underlying routing algorithm must still be
specified.

================================================================================
5.8 Key Design Observation
================================================================================

The detailed study of routing algorithms and Multi-Criteria Path Planning led
to one important observation.

The routing algorithm and the decision-making process are two independent
components.

The routing algorithm determines how the shortest path is computed.

The decision-making process determines how the cost of every road segment is
calculated.

Therefore, the routing algorithm itself does not need to understand weather,
terrain, road conditions, or mission information.

Instead, it only requires meaningful numerical edge weights.

Those edge weights can be generated by a separate decision-making module.

This observation became the foundation of the proposed BorderShield AI routing
architecture.

================================================================================
5.9 Final Design Decision
================================================================================

Based on the comparative analysis, the following architecture was selected.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm is used exclusively for route computation.

A separate Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine evaluates all operational
datasets and calculates a dynamic operational cost for every road segment.

These operational costs become the edge weights of the weighted graph.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm then computes the route having the minimum
cumulative operational cost.

This hybrid architecture combines intelligent decision-making with an
efficient, reliable, and well-established routing algorithm.

================================================================================
5.10 Algorithm Selection Summary
================================================================================

Algorithm / Approach              Decision

Breadth First Search (BFS)        Rejected
Reason: Supports only unweighted routing.

Depth First Search (DFS)          Rejected
Reason: Does not guarantee the optimal path.

Bellman-Ford Algorithm            Rejected
Reason: Negative edge weights are unnecessary.

Floyd-Warshall Algorithm          Rejected
Reason: Computes unnecessary all-pairs routes.

A* Algorithm                      Not Selected
Reason: Additional heuristic complexity is not required.

Standard Dijkstra Algorithm       Selected
Reason: Reliable, efficient, and ideal for weighted routing.

Multi-Criteria Path Planning      Adopted as Design Strategy
Reason: Used for operational cost evaluation instead of path computation.

================================================================================
5.11 Conclusion of Algorithm Selection
================================================================================

After evaluating several routing algorithms and decision-making approaches, it
was concluded that no single algorithm independently satisfies all operational
requirements of BorderShield AI.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm provides an efficient shortest-path solution
but does not consider operational conditions.

Conversely, Multi-Criteria Path Planning enables the evaluation of multiple
operational parameters but does not itself perform shortest-path computation.

Therefore, BorderShield AI adopts a hybrid routing architecture that combines
the strengths of both approaches.

The Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine dynamically computes operational
costs for each road segment, while the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm identifies
the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost.

This combination forms the foundation of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-
Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA), which serves as the core routing framework
of the proposed system.

5.12 Understanding the Routing Architecture
================================================================================

After selecting the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm as the core routing algorithm
and Multi-Criteria Path Planning as the decision-making strategy, the next step
was to determine how both concepts could be integrated into a single routing
framework.

Initially, it appeared that Dynamic Weighted Dijkstra might represent a new
routing algorithm. However, a detailed analysis revealed that this assumption
is technically incorrect.

The routing algorithm itself remains the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path
Algorithm. The innovation of the proposed framework lies not in modifying the
routing algorithm but in dynamically calculating the edge weights before the
routing process begins.

Therefore, the routing architecture consists of two independent components that
work sequentially.

1. Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine (MCREE)

2. Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm

The first component performs intelligent operational analysis, while the second
component performs shortest-path computation.

This separation allows BorderShield AI to remain modular, scalable, and easy to
maintain.

================================================================================
5.13 Difference Between Routing Algorithm and Risk Evaluation
================================================================================

A common misunderstanding is that Dynamic Weighted Dijkstra represents a
completely different shortest-path algorithm.

Technically, this is not correct.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm has remained unchanged for decades and is
responsible only for computing the shortest path within a weighted graph.

It does not understand operational concepts such as:

• Weather

• Terrain

• Road Conditions

• Mission Priority

• Vehicle Type

• Visibility

• Snowfall

• Road Closures

Instead, the algorithm requires only one input for every road segment.

A numerical edge weight.

Its objective is simply to identify the path whose total edge weight is minimum.

Therefore, the intelligence of BorderShield AI is not embedded inside the
Dijkstra Algorithm.

Instead, intelligence is introduced before Dijkstra executes by calculating
meaningful edge weights from multiple operational datasets.

================================================================================
5.14 Relationship Between Risk Evaluation and Routing
================================================================================

The routing framework separates the overall process into two independent stages.

Stage One

Operational evaluation of every road segment.

Stage Two

Shortest-path computation using dynamically generated edge weights.

The relationship between both components is illustrated below.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

        Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine
                     │
                     ▼
     Calculates Operational Cost of Every Road
                     │
                     ▼
       Generates Dynamic Edge Weights
                     │
                     ▼
     Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm
                     │
                     ▼
      Generates Best Operational Route

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This separation ensures that each component performs only one specialized task.

The Risk Evaluation Engine performs intelligent analysis.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm performs efficient routing.

================================================================================
5.15 Formation of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing
      Algorithm (BDMRA)
================================================================================

Based on the architectural study, the proposed routing framework is formed by
integrating the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm with the Multi-Criteria Risk
Evaluation Engine.

The framework can be represented as:

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA)

            =
Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine

            +
Dynamic Edge Weight Generation

            +
Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is important to note that BDMRA is not a newly invented shortest-path
algorithm.

Instead, it is a hybrid routing framework developed specifically for
BorderShield AI by combining an established shortest-path algorithm with a
dynamic operational decision-making mechanism.

================================================================================
5.16 Why Dynamic Edge Weights Are Required
================================================================================

Traditional shortest-path systems assign a fixed weight to every road segment.

For example,

Edge Weight = Distance

Such an approach assumes that road conditions remain constant throughout the
mission.

However, operational environments are highly dynamic.

Road conditions may change because of:

• Weather

• Snowfall

• Terrain Accessibility

• Operational Incidents

• Temporary Road Closures

• Mission Requirements

If the edge weight remains fixed, the routing algorithm cannot adapt to
changing operational conditions.

Therefore, BorderShield AI replaces fixed edge weights with dynamically
generated operational costs.

Instead of using only physical distance,

the proposed framework uses

Edge Weight = Dynamic Operational Cost

The operational cost is calculated using multiple operational datasets and
represents the current suitability of travelling through each road segment.

================================================================================
5.17 Why the Framework Is Called Dynamic
================================================================================

The routing framework is described as dynamic because the edge weights
continuously adapt to changing operational conditions.

Consider a road connecting two operational locations.

Initial Conditions

Weather          : Heavy Snow

Visibility       : Poor

Road Status      : Open

Terrain          : Glacier

The Risk Evaluation Engine computes

Operational Cost = 48

This value becomes the edge weight supplied to the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm.

Later, suppose the weather improves.

Updated Conditions

Weather          : Clear

Visibility       : Good

Road Status      : Open

Terrain          : Glacier

The Risk Evaluation Engine now computes

Operational Cost = 18

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm itself has not changed.

Only the edge weight has changed.

When the routing algorithm executes again, it automatically evaluates the
updated graph and may recommend a completely different operational route.

This automatic adaptation to changing operational conditions is the defining
characteristic of the BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm.

================================================================================
5.19 Advantages of Separating Decision Making and Routing
================================================================================

Separating operational analysis from route computation provides several
important advantages.

• The routing algorithm remains simple and computationally efficient.

• New operational parameters can be added without modifying the routing
  algorithm.

• Dynamic operational conditions automatically influence route
  recommendations.

• The framework follows a modular architecture.

• Risk evaluation logic can evolve independently from the routing algorithm.

• Future datasets can easily be integrated.

• The system becomes easier to maintain, test, and upgrade.

• The architecture remains scalable for future enhancements.

5.20 Technical Interpretation
================================================================================

The BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm should be interpreted
as a routing framework rather than a newly invented shortest-path algorithm.

The framework combines four major components.

• Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation

• Dynamic Operational Cost Generation

• Weighted Graph Construction

• Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Computation

Each component performs a clearly defined responsibility.

Collectively, these components enable intelligent route recommendation under
dynamic operational conditions while preserving the efficiency and reliability
of the Standard Dijkstra Algorithm.

5.21 Official Technical Description
================================================================================

The BorderShield Dynamic Multi-Criteria Routing Algorithm (BDMRA) is a hybrid
routing framework that integrates a Multi-Criteria Risk Evaluation Engine with
the Standard Dijkstra Shortest Path Algorithm.

The Risk Evaluation Engine analyzes operational datasets including Geographic
Master, Road Master, Terrain Master, Weather Master, Mission Information, and
future operational updates to calculate a dynamic operational cost for every
road segment.

These operational costs are assigned as edge weights within a weighted graph.

The Standard Dijkstra Algorithm subsequently processes the dynamically weighted
graph and determines the route having the minimum cumulative operational cost,
thereby recommending the safest and most operationally suitable route between
the selected source and destination.
